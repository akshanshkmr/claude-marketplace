# JMIR Abbreviations, Acronyms, and Initialisms

Complete reference for abbreviation usage, expansion rules, the Abbreviations end section, and the no-expansion list. Based on JMIR House Style §2 and AMA Manual of Style (11th ed, §19.6).

---

## §1. Definitions

- **Abbreviation**: A shortened form of a word or phrase (eg, "Dr" for "Doctor").
- **Acronym**: An abbreviation formed from initial letters and pronounced as a word (eg, "AIDS").
- **Initialism**: An abbreviation formed from initial letters and pronounced letter by letter (eg, "HIV").
- In these guidelines, "abbreviation" is used as a blanket term covering all three.

---

## §2. General Rules for Abbreviation Usage

### 2.1 The ≥3 Rule
All abbreviations (with exceptions in §3) used **3 or more times** must be:
1. Expanded at first mention within each **stand-alone section** of the paper.
2. Format: `expanded name (abbreviation)` — eg, `mobile health (mHealth)`.

For abbreviations used **fewer than 3 times**: use the expanded form only (unless readability is hampered or the abbreviation is better known — see §2.2).

### 2.2 Stand-Alone Sections
Each of the following is considered a separate, independent section — abbreviations must be re-introduced at first mention in each:
- Abstract
- Main text
- End sections (Acknowledgments, Authors' Contributions, Conflicts of Interest, Data Availability)
- Each individual figure
- Each individual table
- Each individual textbox
- Each individual multimedia appendix

**Example**: An abbreviation introduced in the abstract must be reintroduced in the main text using the `expanded form (abbreviation)` format.

### 2.3 Well-Known Abbreviations Exception
Abbreviations for **established terms or entities with lengthy names** that are or will be better known by their abbreviation are allowed regardless of how many times they occur. They should still be expanded at first mention in each stand-alone section. Examples:
- Instruments or scales: `Diabetes Self-Management and Technology Questionnaire (DSMT-Q)`
- Trial names
- Organization names: `ACOG`, `WHO`, `VA`
- Software/technology terms: `REDCap`, `BERT`, `WYSIWYG`
- Reporting standards: `CONSORT`

**Terms currently better known by abbreviation** (eg, PRISMA): list the acronym first, then expand — `PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses)`. This is relatively rare.

**Terms that will be better known by abbreviation** (eg, new instruments): mention expanded version first, then abbreviation — `Diabetes Self-Management Questionnaire (DSMT-Q)`. However, if readability is affected, the copyeditor can relax this and list the abbreviation first.

**Note**: Some instrument names resemble abbreviations but are NOT acronyms. For example, "EQ-5D" (and EQ-5D-3L, EQ-5D-5L, EQ-5D-Y) should not be treated as acronyms.

**Tip**: When in doubt, do a Google or PubMed search, check for a Wikipedia article using the abbreviation, or consult the author.

## §4. Abbreviations in Specific Sections

### 4.1 Title
- Abbreviations are **best avoided** in titles.
- **Exceptions**: Acceptable no-expansion abbreviations (§3); names of technology/apps, collaborations/study groups, or trials; terms better known by abbreviation for discoverability.
- Psychological/medical assessment instruments: expand but also include abbreviation in parentheses.
- Examples: `USMLE` (well-known exam) can stay unexpanded. `NHS` can stay unexpanded.

### 4.2 Keywords
- Well-known abbreviations should be added to keywords to aid indexing.
- If abbreviations are listed as keywords, **include their expanded versions as well**.

### 4.3 Subheadings
- Abbreviations can be used if previously defined in text or on the no-expansion list (§3).
- Avoid a **single abbreviation as a heading** — add a qualifier: `MRI Technique` or `PCR Analysis`, not just `MRI` or `PCR`.

### 4.4 Tables, Figures, Textboxes, and MAs
- Abbreviations often necessary due to space constraints.
- **Tables**: If undefined in table or caption, provide expanded form in a **footnote**, listed in order of appearance.
- **Figures**: If undefined in figure, provide expanded form in the **caption**, listed in alphabetical order.
- **Textboxes**: Expand at first mention within the textbox or its caption.
- **Multimedia appendices**: Include expansion at first mention in the **caption**. Abbreviations within appendix content are NOT the copyeditor's responsibility.
- Abbreviations appearing **exclusively** in tables, figures, or textboxes do **NOT** need to be added to the Abbreviations end section.

### 4.5 End Sections
- Abbreviations in end sections (Conflicts of Interest, Acknowledgments, Authors' Contributions, Data Availability) should be expanded as in the rest of the text.
- The abbreviation may be included parenthetically if the organization is better known as such.
- These do **NOT** need to be added to the Abbreviations end section.

### 4.6 Quoted Text / Blockquotes
- Abbreviations in blockquotes or quoted text do **not** require expansion even if they don't occur elsewhere. Authors may optionally expand in square brackets.

## §5. When to AVOID Abbreviations

### 5.1 Author-Invented Abbreviations
- **Forbidden**: Abbreviations of common words/phrases introduced by the author for convenience.
- Example: "web-based education (WBE)" vs "paper-based education (PBE)" — expand for clarity.
- Exception: See §2.3 for well-known abbreviations of established terms.

### 5.2 People-First / Person-Centered Abbreviations
- **Forbidden**: Do NOT abbreviate people with a condition as "PwX".
- Example: "people with disabilities" is correct — NOT "PwD" (which could mean people with disabilities, dementia, diabetes, dogs, etc).
- This is counter-productive to person-first language.
- Exception: Abbreviation of people may be acceptable in tables with severe space constraints.

### 5.3 Person-Centered Demographic Abbreviations
- **Forbidden**: "African American women" should NOT be abbreviated as "AAW."

### 5.4 One-Word Terms
- Should NOT be abbreviated: "Patient" ≠ "Pt", "equation" ≠ "eqn."
- **Exception**: Lengthy single words with well-known abbreviations can be retained: `ECG` (electrocardiogram), `TB` (tuberculosis).

## §6. The Abbreviations End Section

### 6.1 What to Include
- **Only** abbreviations used in the **abstract or main text** (excluding other stand-alone sections like tables, figures, end sections).
- Include **regardless of the number of times used** in the abstract/main text.
- Abbreviations from the no-expansion list (§3) that are used in abstract or main text: **compile them in the end section** regardless of frequency.

### 6.2 Heading
- Use **H3 heading style**: `Abbreviations`

### 6.3 Sorting Order
Preferred alphabetical order with the following priority:
1. **Punctuation** (eg, `-likely`)
2. **Symbols** (eg, `.NET`, `/kg`, `$delta`)
3. **Numerals** (eg, `1-FU`, `5-BP`)
4. **Plain text A-Z** (eg, `AMA`, `STD`)
5. **Lowercase Greek letters** (eg, `α-KG`, `αSMA`, `β-CA`, `ω-HA`)

### 6.4 Example

```
Abbreviations
-likely: something-likely
.NET: dot NET
/kg: per kilogram
$delta: difference in USD values
1-FU: 5-fluorouracil
5-BP: 1-bromophenol
AMA: American Medical Association
STD: sexually transmitted disease
α-KG: α-ketoglutarate
αSMA: α-smooth muscle actin
β-CA: β-catenin
ω-HA: ω-bromohexanoic acid
```

### 6.5 Position in Manuscript
- Appears as the **last end section** before the References.
- In the PDF, the Abbreviations section should appear **after the References**.

## §7. Other Abbreviation Guidelines

### 7.1 AI and mHealth
- **Expand** `AI` (artificial intelligence) and `mHealth` (mobile health) at first mention.
- Add both to the Abbreviations end section.
- Both can be used **without expansion only in the article title**.
- `eHealth` does NOT need to be expanded anywhere.

### 7.2 Country Names
- **Spell out** when used as a noun: "There are 78 million dogs in the United States."
- **Abbreviate** when used as an adjective: "All US dogs must be vaccinated."
- Australian state abbreviations (eg, "NSW, Australia") are allowed in author affiliations per AMA.

### 7.3 "Stretched" Study Names / Acronyms
- When a study name is an acronym derived from non-initial letters of the phrase (eg, "VIGILANCE" from "VItal siGns monItoring with continuous puLse oximetry..."):
  - List the acronym first with the phrase in parentheses (reversed order).
  - Do NOT use unusual capitalization to show derivation.
  - Spelled-out form should be in **title case**: `VIGILANCE (Vital Signs Monitoring With Continuous Pulse Oximetry and Wireless Clinician Notification After Surgery)`

### 7.4 Product Names Resembling Acronyms
- Do NOT expand proper nouns that resemble acronyms but aren't (eg, `iOS`).
- Do NOT expand where the abbreviated version is the official presentation (eg, `NHS England`).

### 7.5 JMIR as Abbreviation
- Do NOT expand "JMIR" for the publisher ("JMIR Publications") or sister journals ("JMIR Mental Health").
- Expand to "Journal of Medical Internet Research" ONLY when referring to the specific journal at jmir.org (ISSN 1438-8871).

### 7.6 Table Header Convention
- If "No. of Participants" appears in a column header, change to `Participants, n (%)`.
- In Acknowledgments, change "Grant No." to `grant XXXXX` per AMA.

## §8. Latin Abbreviations

| Abbreviation | Meaning | Rules |
|-------------|---------|-------|
| eg, | for example | No italics, no periods. Use only in parentheses. Follow with comma. Use "for example" in running text. |
| ie, | that is | No italics, no periods. Use only in parentheses. Follow with comma. Use "that is" in running text. |
| et al | and others | No italics, no period. Can be used in running text. |
| etc | et cetera | No italics, no period. |
| vs | versus | No italics, no period. Can be used in running text (unlike eg/ie). |

**Examples**: `(ie, ...)`, `(eg, ...)`, `Smith et al [15]`, `etc`, `treatment vs control`

---

## §9. Abbreviations of Names or Titles

- Do NOT include periods with honorifics, scientific terms, or abbreviations.
- **"St."** is OK in a person's name: `Martin St. Croix`
- Do NOT use a period in city names with "St": `St Louis, Missouri`
- **"St."** in organization names: use period if it's in the official name (eg, `St. Jude Hospital`)
