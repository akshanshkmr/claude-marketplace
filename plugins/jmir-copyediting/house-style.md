# JMIR House Style and Editorial Guidelines

Complete reference for language, spelling, punctuation, capitalization, numbers, time, trademarks, nomenclature, lists, and blockquotes. Based on JMIR House Style and AMA Manual of Style (11th ed).

---

## §1. Style and Language

### 1.1 Editing Level
- **Light to moderate** editing only. Correct grammar, spelling, run-on sentences, comma splices, awkward phrasing, superfluous/casual language.
- Do NOT perform heavy stylistic rewriting or move text around to improve flow.
- Preserve the author's original voice. Manuscripts have already passed peer review.
- Non-native English speaker manuscripts may require more extensive attention.

### 1.2 Voice and Person
> **Bulletin 9 (Jun 2026):** JMIR removed the long-standing restrictions against **passive voice** and **first-person pronouns** (they were not based on AMA or general convention). Do **not** convert passive→active or first-person→neutral as a style rule. Edit these only for genuine clarity, never for conformity.
- **Passive voice**: acceptable. No longer flag or convert it on principle. (Prefer active only where the passive genuinely obscures who did what.)
- **First person plural** ("we sampled...") is acceptable. Light touch if it becomes repetitive, but do not force it away.
- **First person singular** ("I", "my"): acceptable, including in opinion pieces and Discussion. No longer forbidden — do not rewrite to "It is the opinion of this author."

### 1.3 Tense
| Section | Tense | Example |
|---------|-------|---------|
| Methods | Past tense | "This study was conducted..." |
| Methods (Protocols/Proposals) | Future or present | "Participants will be enrolled..." / "The study is currently recruiting..." |
| Objective (study is subject) | Present preferred | "This paper explores/describes/presents..." |
| Objective (completed research) | Past acceptable | "We aimed to determine..." / "The objective was to..." |

### 1.4 Self-Reference
> **Bulletin 9 (Jun 2026):** The restriction against the phrase "the current article" (and self-reference wording generally) was removed. Do **not** change "present study" / "current study" / "current paper" / "current article" → "this study." Leave the author's self-reference as written.
- Self-referential wording ("present study", "current study", "current paper", "current article") is acceptable — no longer edited.

### 1.5 Spelling
- **American English** always. Follow AMA Style Guide.
- Examples: `color` (not colour), `behavior` (not behaviour), `pediatric` (not paediatric), `enrollment` (not enrolment).

### 1.6 SMS vs Text Messaging
- **Titles and headings**: "text messaging" or "texting" preferred.
- **Abstract**: Use both — "SMS text messaging interventions."
- **Body text**: Prefer "texting" or "SMS text messaging."

### 1.7 Use of "and/or"
- As of December 2025: retain "and/or" as is. No longer required to rephrase.

### 1.8 Paper vs Article
- "Paper" or "study" is preferred, but per Bulletin 9 (§1.4) self-reference is no longer policed — do not force-change "article" to "paper."

## §3. Headings, Titles, and Document Structure

### 3.1 General Rule
Use **headline style caps (title case)** for ALL titles and headings.

### 3.2 Words NOT Capitalized in Title Case
- Articles: a, an, the
- Prepositions of ≤3 letters: of, as, in, to, at, by, for, on, up
- Coordinating conjunctions: and, or, for, nor, but
- The "to" in infinitives
- **Exception**: Capitalize if it is the first word in the title or subtitle.
- **Do capitalize** 2-letter verbs: Is, Be, Do, Go.

### 3.3 Special Capitalization Rules

| Situation | Rule | Example |
|-----------|------|---------|
| Hyphenated compounds | Capitalize both parts | `Population-Based Study`, `Drug-Resistant Bacteria`, `Self-Referral` |
| Prefixed compounds in titles | Capitalize both parts | `Anti-Inflammatory Drugs`, `Cross-Sectional Study` |
| Non-proper names starting with lowercase | Do NOT capitalize | `An mHealth Intervention for People With Dementia...` |
| e- compounds in titles | **Capitalize the E-** (Bulletin 9: the "e-" exception was removed to align with AMA) | `E-Cigarettes and Future Battles...`, `E-Mental Health`, `E-Learning` |
| Intercapped trade names | Keep original casing | `eBay`, `iPhone` in titles and headings |
| After lowercase Greek letter | Capitalize next word | `Systemic Adverse Effects of Ophthalmic β-Blockers` |
| After numeral (not unit) | Capitalize next word | `High-Dose 308-nm Excimer Laser` |
| After organic prefix (cis-, trans-) | Capitalize next word | `Concurrent Use of cis-Platinum` |
| Genus name | Capitalize genus, lowercase species | `Helicobacter pylori and the Patient With Ulcers` |
| Foreign compound terms | Exception for whole phrase | `Ethical Questions Surrounding In Vitro Fertilization` |
| Phrasal verbs | Exception for particle | `Weighing In on Bariatric Surgery` |

### 3.4 App/Software Names in Titles
- If an app name stylistically starts lowercase (eg, "headspace Work and Study"), capitalize it in titles: "Headspace Work and Study."
- Exception: eHealth/mHealth neologisms keep their lowercase prefix.

### 3.5 Guidelines for Article Titles
* **Character Limit**: All titles must fit within a **280-character limit** (including spaces), be descriptive, not reveal results, be concise, and be understandable to those outside the field.
* **Standard Format**: `"Issue/Intervention in Demographic/Disease/Condition: Method/Study Design"`
  * *Example*: `Continuous Remote Patient Monitoring in Patients With Heart Failure (Cascade Study): Protocol for a Mixed Methods Feasibility Study`
* **Title Requirements by Article Type**:

  | Article Type | Title Guidance |
  |---|---|
  | Original Paper (including systematic reviews, RCTs, pilot studies) | Descriptive, includes study type and research question, in title case |
  | Protocols/Proposals | Must contain "Protocol for a [Study Design]" or "Proposal for a [Study Design]" in the second half |
  | Reviews | Descriptive, includes study type. "Systematic Review" only if strictly following PRISMA statement |
  | Tutorials | Must contain "Tutorial" in the second part |
  | Viewpoints / Research Letters | Descriptive, declarative, or interrogative. No study design required |
  | Commentaries | Descriptive, declarative, or interrogative. Avoid "A Commentary" as a stand-alone subtitle. Do NOT include the title of the original publication |
  | Letters to the Editor | Short, unique, descriptive title. Do NOT include the title of the original publication |
  | Author's Reply | Use format `Authors' Reply: [title of the Letter to the Editor]` |
  | Corrigenda | Must contain the name of the article being corrected and the word "Correction" |
  | In Memoriam | Must contain "In Memoriam" followed by the individual's name |

* **Title Types**:
  * **Descriptive** (preferred for original papers, reviews, protocols): Describes the subject and method/design. Does NOT reveal main conclusions.
  * **Declarative** (acceptable for viewpoints/commentaries/letters): States the main findings or conclusions. Discouraged for original articles.
  * **Interrogative** (requires approval for original articles): Introduces the subject as a question.
* **Study Design/Method**:
  * **RCTs**: Title must contain "**Randomized Controlled Trial**" in the second half.
  * **Protocols/Proposals**: Title must contain "**Protocol for a [Study Design]**" or "**Proposal for a [Study Design]**" in the second half.
  * **Viewpoints and Commentaries**: Do not need a study design in the title.
* **Punctuation**: Do not use more than one colon in a title (except corrigenda).
* **Formatting**: Do not use special formatting (such as italics, subscripts, superscripts) in the article title — typesetting scripts do not allow it.
* **Subtitles**: Omit indefinite articles in the study design subtitle (eg, use `...: Randomized Controlled Trial`, NOT `...: A Randomized Controlled Trial`).
* **Acronyms and Abbreviations**:
  * Avoid abbreviations in titles, except standard ones (`AI`, `WHO`, `NIH`, `CDC`, `FDA`, `USMLE`, etc.) or study acronyms.
  * If a study acronym is derived from a "stretch" of words (eg, VIGILANCE), use regular Title Case for the spelled-out form: `VIGILANCE (Vital Signs Monitoring With Continuous Pulse Oximetry and Wireless Clinician Notification After Surgery)`.
* **Descriptive Titles**: Avoid using only a brand or app name. Prompt authors to add a descriptive phrase first, and put the brand name in parentheses.
  * *Avoid*: `Acceptability of Autobot: Randomized Controlled Trial`
  * *Preferred*: `A Robot for Autistic Children (Autobot): Randomized Controlled Trial`
* **Geographic Locations**: Only include country or geographic names (eg, `...in Japan...`) in the title if essential to the findings or context (eg, low- and middle-income countries, or results heavily dependent on geography). Otherwise, move geography mentions to the abstract/Methods.
* **Systematic Reviews**: Only use "Systematic Review" if the study strictly follows the PRISMA statement. App store searches or website assessments must NOT be called "Systematic Review" (use `Systematic Search on App Stores and Content Analysis` or similar instead).
* **Revision Rule**: Make all title suggestions/changes during the initial Step 1 copyediting round. Directly edit the title in the Word document. **Bulletin 9 (Jun 2026) — metadata handling changed:** on **Kriyadocs**, copyeditors should **no longer edit author metadata** (including the title field); forward any needed change to the production team via a PQ. On **OJS**, record copyeditor metadata modifications in the layout notes for production review (and, as the transition rolls out, authors relay their own metadata changes to you as comments rather than editing the form). If authors refuse a title change, pass information to the Production Editor via Layout Note.

### 3.6 Document Heading Levels & Structure
* **Platform heading levels**:
  * **OJS (Open Journal Systems)**: Main section headings (Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, References) are styled as **Heading 2 (H2)**. First-level subheadings are **Heading 3 (H3)**, and successive subheadings use H4, H5, and H6.
  * **Kriyadocs**: Main section headings are styled starting from **Heading 1 (H1)**. First-level subheadings are Heading 2 (H2), and successive subheadings use H3, H4, etc.
* **Hierarchy Rules**:
  * Subheadings must follow logical sequential levels (eg, H2 followed by H3, not jumping straight to H4).
  * Avoid soft line breaks (`Shift+Enter`) or extra line breaks (`Enter`) between headings and paragraphs.
* **Multiplicity (Rule of 2)**:
  * A section should have a **minimum of 2 subheadings** of the same level if subheadings are used. Having only a single subheading under a primary section is forbidden. If only one exists, either add a second subheading or delete the single subheading.
  * *Example (Discussion)*: Discussion sections often end with a "`Conclusions`" subheading. To satisfy the Rule of 2, you must insert a subheading at the start of the Discussion (eg, `Principal Findings`) so there are at least two H3 subheadings (eg, `Principal Findings` and `Conclusions`).
  * *Position of Conclusions*: `Conclusions` must be the last H3/H2 subheading of the Discussion section.
* **Introductory paragraphs**: A primary heading (eg, H2) can be followed by a short introductory paragraph (1-2 sentences) before the first subheading. However, a long paragraph is not acceptable without a subheading.
* **Ethical Considerations subheading**:
  * All studies involving humans, medical records, patient data, observations of public behavior, or secondary data analyses must have an **Ethical Considerations** subheading.
  * Position: Place this section **within the Methods section** (never in Results).
  * Level: Styled as **H3** (in OJS) or **H2** (in Kriyadocs).
  * Heading Name: Use `Ethical Considerations` (use `Ethics Approval` only if only ethics approval info with associated identifiers is provided).
* **Results section for Protocols and Proposals**:
  * Since research protocols/proposals do not have results, the section is often missing.
  * **Rule**: You must still include a **Results** heading (H2/H1 level). Ask the author to add 1-2 sentences stating the funding and enrollment status or when results are expected.
  * *Template*: `"The project was funded in YYYY, and enrollment was completed in [month YYYY]. Data analysis is currently underway, and the first results are expected to be submitted for publication in YYYY."`
  * Reformat the abstract to follow the standard Background-Objectives-Methods-Results-Conclusions structure, using a similar placeholder sentence for Results/Conclusions.
* **Citations in Headings**: Avoid citing figures, tables, or references inside a heading or subheading. Move the citation to the text.
* **Atypical Structures**:
  * Atypical structures (eg, blended `Methods and Results` or `Results and Discussion`) are allowed if logically warranted for all journals **except the main Journal of Medical Internet Research**.
  * Atypical structures must be approved by the Editorial Team (and will be noted in the submission notes/assignment email).

### 3.7 Job Titles (New — Bulletin 9, Jun 2026)
JMIR added guidance on capitalizing job titles. Follow AMA (the skill's fallback where JMIR text isn't quoted verbatim):
- **Capitalize** a formal title only when it **immediately precedes** a personal name as part of the name: `Director Jane Smith`, `Professor Lee`.
- **Lowercase** the title when it **follows** the name or **stands alone** in running text: `Jane Smith, director of the clinic`; `the professor`; `the principal investigator`.
- Do not capitalize a title merely because the role is important. (If a specific manuscript quotes different JMIR wording via a "CE:" note, that wording wins.)

## §4. Hyperlinks
- **Remove all hyperlinks** from text and abstract (they cause typesetting issues).
- If URLs are cited, add them as formal references before loading into RefCheck.
- **Exception**: Direct quotes containing a URL can keep it if it doesn't affect kerning. If it does, replace with `[link]`.

## §5. Indefinite Articles
- Use **"an"** with abbreviations that begin with vowel sounds, even if the spelled-out form uses "a", and vice versa.
- Examples: an MD degree, a UV source, an SMS diary, an RCT, a URL.

---

## §6. Italics
- Do NOT use italics for titles in the reference list.
- Do NOT italicize words considered part of English language (eg, in vivo, in vitro).
- **Italicize** these statistical terms: *P*, *r*, *t*, *U*, *F*, *d*, *g*, *R²*, *η²*.
- Italicize "sic."
- Italics can be used in text **sparingly** for emphasis.
- Blockquotes are styled in italics.
- Software package names are italicized (eg, *tidyverse*).
- Biological binomials (genus + species) are italicized (eg, *Helicobacter pylori*).

## §7. Numbers

### 7.1 General Guidelines
- Use **numerals** for numbers in most circumstances (eg, "3 questionnaires").
- Do NOT automatically spell out numbers less than 10 — consider context.
- Do NOT spell out numbers in a quantitative results context.
- **Bulletin 9 (Jun 2026):** numeral rules were streamlined to AMA. Numerals are generally preferred; the spelled-out cases in §7.2 still apply but are now at the **discretion of the author or copyeditor** in borderline cases — do not over-police a defensible choice.

### 7.2 When to Use Numerals vs Words

**Use NUMERALS when:**
| Context | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Countable quantity | "3 questionnaires", "9 male and 6 female participants" |
| Measurement with unit | "79.5 kg", "75%", "8 weeks", "5 minutes" |
| Statistics / directionality | "2-sided alternate hypothesis", "2-tailed t test" |
| Clock positions | "the 8-o'clock position" |
| Ordinals ≥10th | "15th experiment", "5th and 10th grades" |
| Large numbers | "2.5 million patient records", "200,000 patients" |

**Use WORDS when:**
| Context | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Beginning of sentence | "Nine of the included studies..." (or reword) |
| Before in-sentence numbered list | "This study has two objectives: (1)..." |
| Used as pronouns | "One could assume that..." |
| Two numerals back-to-back | "five 16-day cycles" |
| Common fractions | "approximately one-third" |
| Ordinals first–ninth | "the first group", "the second phase" |
| Common adjectival phrases | "a one-size-fits-all approach" |
| Common phrases | "one of the most versatile tools" |
| Unintended emphasis on quantity | "two methods", "three common formats" |
| Idiomatic expressions | "one key factor" |

**Note**: "st", "nd", "rd", "th" ordinal suffixes are NOT superscripted.

### 7.3 Negative Numbers
- Use an **en dash** (–) for the minus/negative sign. Acceptable substitute for the minus sign (−).
- Do NOT use a hyphen (-) as a minus sign.
- Ensure consistent use of the same character throughout the paper.
- Watch for rendering issues in PDFs — replace minus signs (−) with en dashes if needed.

### 7.4 Percentages
- Use the percent sign **without a space**: `58%`
- **Repeat** the percent sign in ranges: `24%-29%`; `between 50% and 55%` (NOT `24-29%` or `between 50 and 55%`).
- JMIR requires **n/N values** alongside percentages in running text: `Of the 80% (40/50) of participants...`
- In **tables**, the percent sign appears ONLY in the column header (eg, "Participants, n (%)"), not in the table body.
- If tables contain only percentages without absolute values, ask authors to provide n values or a footnote explaining why they cannot.

### 7.5 Decimals
- Use decimal format for numbers with units of measure.
- Values < 1 require a **leading zero** before the decimal point — **except** P, α, and β values (eg, `P<.001` but `0.7 g`).
- Percentages for N≥1000: 2 decimal points are allowed but not required. If authors provide 1 decimal, no need to request adjustment. Ensure consistency.

### 7.6 Number Spacing (Thousands Separator)
- Numbers > 9999: use comma separator.
- Examples: `36,127`; `250,000`; `1,030,023`
- Numbers ≤ 9999: no separator. Example: `6500`

### 7.7 Spacing Around Equality/Inequality Signs
- Do **NOT** insert spaces around `=`, `<`, `>`, `≤`, `≥` in statistical expressions.
- Examples: `P<.001`, `n=12`, `t15=2.68`
- Note: Our typesetting scripts auto-remove these spaces, so copyeditors do not need to spend time on this.
- **Exception**: Spaces ARE used in complex standalone equations (eg, `y = C - c`).

### 7.8 Units of Measure
- SI units recommended. Abbreviate when used with numerals, in parenthetical text, and in virgule constructions.
- Time abbreviations (d, h, ms, min, mo, s, wk, y): use only in virgule constructions, parenthetical text, tables/figures/textboxes. Spell out in running text.
- Unit symbols are NOT pluralized: `78.5 kg` (not `78.5 kgs`).
- Example: "The participants exercised daily (aerobics: 25 min)" vs "The participants exercised daily for 25 minutes..."

### 7.9 Temperature
- Use Celsius scale (°C). Convert Fahrenheit (°F) values to °C.
- Add a **space** between numeral and unit: `37.5 °C`

### 7.10 Time
- Report time conventionally (12-hour clock), NOT European/military (24-hour).
- Use "AM" and "PM" in regular capitals (not small caps).
- Report "12 PM" as **"noon"** and "12 AM" as **"midnight"**.
- ":00" is not required for times on the hour.
- Examples: `7 PM`, `10:30 AM`
- **Exception**: 24-hour clock is acceptable when specifically referencing literature from the study.

### 7.11 Eras
- Use AD/BC or CE/BCE consistently (not interchangeably).
- AD precedes the year: `AD 2018`. BC/CE/BCE follow: `400 BC`, `2018 CE`.
- Use regular capitals (not small caps).

## §8. Punctuation

### 8.1 Serial Comma
- **Always use** the serial (Oxford) comma.
- Example: "children, teens, and adults."

### 8.2 Ellipsis Points
- Do NOT use Word's ellipsis symbol (…). Use **three periods** (...).
- No spaces before or after: `This was...already discussed.`

### 8.3 Em Dash
- **No space** before or after an em dash.
- Example: "All these medications—antidepressants, barbiturates, antipsychotics, and sedatives—must be used with caution."

### 8.4 Hyphen Rules

**No hyphen with these prefixes** (unless preceding a proper noun/acronym):
> anti, ante, bi, co, contra, counter, de, extra, infra, inter, intra, macro, meso, micro, mid, multi, neo, non, over, peri, pre, post, pro, pseudo, re, semi, sub, super, supra, trans, tri, ultra, un, under

**Exceptions requiring a hyphen:**
- Compound could be misread: `co-design` (not `codesign`, which looks like "code" + "sign")
- Two same vowels back-to-back: `re-evaluate`
- Three same consonants back-to-back: `hull-less`, `bell-like`

**En dash (not hyphen) for:**
- Attaching modifiers to compound words: `mobile phone–based app` (NOT `mobile phone-based app`)

**Hyphens for ranges:**
- Use hyphens (not en dashes) for numerical ranges: `10-25`
- When one value is negative: use "to" instead of hyphen: `–5 to 10`

**Other hyphen rules:**
- Do NOT use a hyphen as a minus sign (use en dash or Word's minus sign).
- eHealth neologisms: `eHealth`, `mHealth`, `eSource` — never `e-health`, `m-Health`, etc.
- Consult Merriam-Webster for word breaks.
- In the absence of a rule, use hyphens to avoid ambiguity.

### 8.5 Parentheses
- Numbered lists within a sentence use parentheses: `(1) to assess..., (2) to analyze...`
- "ie," and "eg," phrases must be in parentheses: `(eg, green, blue, orange)`

### 8.6 Quotation Marks
- Do NOT use quotation marks for titles in the reference list.
- Use double quotation marks for short quotes within text.
- Single quotation marks for quotes within quotes only.
- Example: `One participant said, "You have to want to succeed before the app can help you" (P12, male, 75 years old).`

## §9. Lists

### 9.1 Bulleted Lists
- Use **sparingly** and only if absolutely necessary.
- Convert short bullet items (eg, inclusion/exclusion criteria, single-word items) into full sentences where possible.
- End punctuation at end of each point only if it forms a complete sentence.
- Do NOT indent lists in Word — scripts handle this during typesetting.

### 9.2 Numbered Lists
- Use sparingly. Arabic numerals followed by a period.
- Numbered lists within a sentence: numerals in parentheses: `(1)`, `(2)`, `(3)`.
- Numbering restarts at "1" in each new section.
- For longer numbered paragraphs: outline first, then use ordinals (first, second, third).

---

## §10. Blockquotes

### When to Use Blockquotes
- Quotes longer than 1 sentence
- Multiple quotes in succession (eg, from different interview participants)
- A quote at the very beginning of an article as an introduction

### Formatting
- **Italicized and indented**, no quotation marks.
- Speaker/patient identifier at end in **square brackets (not italic)**: `[Patient #24, male]`. **Bulletin 9 (Jun 2026): identifiers are optional** — do not require or add them if the author omitted them.
- Do NOT use bullet points for successive quotes.
- Short singular quotes use **in-sentence quotation marks** instead.
- If >⅓ of a page is quotes: suggest putting them in a textbox or table (columns: "Theme/category" and "Illustrative quote").

### Conversational Blockquotes
- For back-and-forth quotes: entire conversation in one blockquote.
- Each speaker identified with **bold initials** (or "Interviewer"/"Author") followed by a colon.
- Defer to author preference on identifiers.

## §11. Trademarks and Service Marks

- **Remove all ®, ™, ℠ symbols** from scholarly articles.
- Remove wrongly used © symbols after names/terms.
- **Capitalize the initial letter** of trademarked terms instead.
- Per AMA §5.6.15.9: initial capital + all lowercase (eg, `Xerox`, `Kodak`, `Scopus`, `Embase`) unless:
  - Abbreviation: `IBM`, `JAMA`, `DSM-5`
  - Intercapped construction: `PubMed`, `iTunes`
  - Trademarked online databases: all caps OK (eg, `MEDLINE`, `CINAHL`, `SCIE`)

---

## §12. Equipment, Reagents, Devices, and Software

- Nonproprietary names preferred. If proprietary names are needed, include **manufacturer/developer in parentheses** at first mention.
- If developer name is already part of the software name, parenthetical can be omitted.
- Location of manufacturer is **no longer required** (easy to look up).
- Examples:
  - `...using a triaxial GT3x+ accelerometer (ActiGraph).`
  - `...using SPSS software (version 20.0; IBM Corp).`
  - `...using R software (version 4.1.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing), including the tidyverse package (version 1.3.2)...`
  - `Microsoft Excel was used to manage and analyze the data.`
- Note: Software **package names** are italicized (eg, *tidyverse*).

## §13. Nomenclature

### 13.1 Biological Nomenclature
- Binomial (genus + species) is always **italicized**: *Homo sapiens*
- **Genus**: capitalized. **Species**: lowercase.
- Expand at first use in each manuscript section (title, abstract, text).
- After first use, abbreviate genus (no period): *H sapiens*
- Never abbreviate the species name alone.
- Do not abbreviate the genus when used alone.
- Do not begin a sentence with an abbreviated genus — expand or reword.

### 13.2 Virus Nomenclature
- Formal virus genus and species names: **italicized**.
- Viral taxa above genus: NOT italicized (JAMA convention).
- Vernacular/common names: NOT italicized (eg, `West Nile virus`).
- Terms ending in -virales, -viridae, -virinae: **capitalized** (eg, `Parvoviridae`).
- Terms ending in -virus: may be formal or common — query author if unsure.
- Give formal taxonomic identity at first mention; informal name can be used thereafter.

---

## §14. Race and Ethnicity
- Capitalize **"Black"** and **"White"** (adjective) when describing race (per latest AMA guidelines).

---

## §15. Socioeconomic Description of Countries
- **Correct**: limited-income, low-income, resource-limited, resource-poor, transitional
- **Incorrect**: first-world/third-world, developed/developing
